Sunlight zone · 0–200 m

The part everyone knows.

Almost all ocean life depends on this thin, lit layer. By its lower edge, one percent of the surface light is left.

Every ten metres of water adds another atmosphere of pressure. Watch the gauge.

Midnight zone · 1,000–4,000 m

No sun from here down.

Three quarters of the animals down here make their own light. With the floodlights on, you won't see any of them.

The vessel · holding at 4,000 m

HTI-01 Tenebra

  1. 01

    Crew sphere

    Forged Ti-6Al-4V titanium, 2.1 m inside, 94 mm wall. Three people, three viewports. Tested to 13,100 m.

  2. 02

    Syntactic foam

    11.4 m³ of hollow glass microspheres set in epoxy, under a lift-off fairing. Buoyancy that doesn't crush at full ocean depth.

  3. 03

    Thrusters

    A 12 kW ducted main drive with an X-tail, plus four 5 kW manoeuvring thrusters. Cruise at 1.5 knots on the bottom.

  4. 04

    Frame & power

    Titanium skids, two 30 kWh battery pods in oil, 420 kg of steel drop-weight for the ride home.

  5. 05

    Bow

    Two 30,000-lumen LED floods, a seven-function manipulator, a sample basket, and a colour chart for the camera.

Abyssal zone · 4,000–6,000 m

Mapped by sound.

No light reaches the floor, so we listen for it. Each ping draws a little more.

Abyssal plains cover about half the planet. They're among the flattest places on it, and among the least seen.
